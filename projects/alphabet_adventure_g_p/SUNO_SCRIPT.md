# Alphabet Adventure — G to P

Audio script for the next video, continuing after the completed A–F lesson.
Use Suno Custom mode, with vocals enabled. This version follows the first video's
spoken lesson format with gentle musical accompaniment.

## Title

Alphabet Adventure — G to P | Ten New Letters

## Style prompt

Warm English spoken-word preschool lesson for children aged 3–5, with very soft,
playful musical accompaniment. One friendly adult female narrator, clear diction,
patient kindergarten-teacher delivery, relaxed conversational pace around 140–155
words per minute. Quiet ukulele, soft piano and occasional delicate xylophone notes.
Keep the voice much louder than the music.

Teach English letter names, not isolated phonics. Follow the supplied script and
letter order exactly: G, H, I, J, K, L, M, N, O, P. Speak all ten letters distinctly.
Use natural warmth and gentle encouragement. Leave about two seconds after
repeat-after-me questions and about three seconds before quiz answers.

Spoken narration throughout, no rap, no overlapping voices, no fast delivery,
no loud percussion, no dramatic sound effects and no added lyrics.
Do not speak section labels or bracketed stage directions.
Aim for approximately three to four minutes, with a gentle ending.

## Recording notes

- Suno may sing some lines or shorten the requested pauses. Check that every letter
  and word is present, in order, and that bracketed directions are not spoken.
- Export the complete audio as MP3 or WAV and provide its local file path.
- The video will be timed to the actual recording rather than a guessed duration.
- The three quiz answers must remain **M, K, P**, respectively.
- The lesson teaches letter names and vocabulary, not individual letter sounds.

## Optional style alternatives

Keep the same script and order if changing the audio style:

1. **Rhythmic spoken lesson:** a light nursery-rhyme beat under clear spoken narration,
   still with only one lead voice and space for responses.
2. **Gentle sing-speak lesson:** a simple recurring melody for each letter-word pair,
   with spoken questions and spoken quiz answers. This will usually need a different
   video timeline from the recommended narration.
